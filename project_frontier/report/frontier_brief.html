Project Frontier · decode-accelerator feasibility study

Can a 3D-DRAM decode accelerator serve frontier MoE models at 1,000–2,000 tokens/s per user — at GPU-class cost?

A full-stack simulation study of Fabrik on the ATLAS (MICRO '26) infrastructure: an independent analytical model validated ±20% against cycle-level simulation, Ramulator-measured memory behavior, HotSpot thermal checks, and a 64,800-point design-space sweep over DeepSeek V4-Flash and MiniMax M3 at batch 1–4, contexts to 1M tokens.

01 — the physics

Decode at batch 1–4 is a bandwidth problem, and expert weights don't amortize

V4-Flash moves 11.2 GB per token at batch 1 — and the surprise is its composition: dense attention-side projections (4.6B parameters at head-geometry 64×512) outweigh the FP4 routed experts. M3 moves 26 GB/token (fp8). Statistical routing gives just 3–4% expert-weight overlap at batch 4, so a bandwidth-first machine is the right shape. At 1M context the growth terms differ: V4's Lightning-Indexer scan (0.37 GB/token); M3's three full-attention layers (3.1 GB/token).

02 — the design space

Bandwidth is the only first-order axis at batch 1

TPS contours are nearly vertical at B=1: 0.25–0.5 PFLOP/s of FP8 keeps up with the stream. Contours bend at batch 4 and 1M context, where attention/indexer compute rises 4.4×. Minimum hardware for the targets: V4-Flash reaches 1,000 TPS at 24 TB/s and 2,000 at 40 TB/s (B=1); M3-fp8 needs 40 and 100 TB/s respectively.

03 — the frontier position

An unoccupied region: 2,000+ TPS/user at ≤$0.17 per million tokens

Measured GPU serving of V4-Flash today delivers 107–306 TPS/user at $0.17–0.66/1M output tokens. Cerebras publishes 1,800–4,400 TPS on smaller dense models (no wafer-scale offering of these MoEs exists). The Fabrik flagship sits at 2,560 TPS / $0.16 (B=1) and 1,484 TPS / $0.072 (B=4) — with $50M–400M of silicon NRE amortizing at 1k–10k units.

04 — the physical checks

Validated, calibrated, thermally sane

The analytical model reproduces ATLAS cycle-level end-to-end latency within 0.82×/1.16× (dense/MoE) with zero tuning. Every access pattern is priced at Ramulator-measured efficiency — sequential streams 0.80 of peak, V4's scattered index-row gathers only 0.29. HotSpot puts the flagship at 84–92°C on liquid cooling. Energy is DRAM-array-dominated (~55%) — the memory bill is paid, not hidden.

06 — addendum: the MLA boundary

The thesis is generation-dependent — and the algorithm side is moving toward it

A cross-validated DeepSeek-V3 (671B/37B) characterization draws the boundary sharply. V3's multi-head latent attention runs at 484 FLOP/byte on its shared latent stream: attention crosses over to dominate decode FLOPs at ~4,400 tokens of context and is 97% of them at 128K. On a bandwidth-first card that makes dense-MLA long context compute-bound — V3 reaches only ~520 TPS/user at 128K even on 105 TB/s — while at 2 PFLOP/s that attention can consume just 4.1 TB/s of the memory system. Two consequences: provision bandwidth per operator by arithmetic intensity, never byte share (experts get the vaults; attention gets the tile array); and the V4-Flash generation, whose compressed attention caps both KV reads and attention FLOPs, is exactly the workload Fabrik should be built for. Balance check: a 128 GB / 105 TB/s card (1.2 GB per TB/s) is balanced for batch-16 serving; interactive B=1–4 wants 5–18 GB per TB/s — the flagship's 256/50 sits there.
